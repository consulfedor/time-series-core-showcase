    01 / PERCEPTION · UNDERSTANDING · MEMORY

    See the whole. Find what matters.

    What matters shapes every next useful step: what the desired picture still lacks, why this matters now, and which change can bring us closer to the whole vision. This helps focus attention on what brings us closer to the goal, reduce cognitive load and time spent searching or handling routine tasks, and make each next step more effective.

    A person perceives, interprets, identifies relationships and assigns meaning. Memory preserves Experience and Knowledge. Memory systems retrieve relevant meaning, sources and connections for the current question and goal. The agent helps compare alternatives against requirements, evidence and constraints. Having completed the work, the agent submits the result for validation: it demonstrates progress toward the goal, checks its quality and confirms the actual outcome through the shared observation point. The person examines the evidence, validates and accepts the result.

    Importance is always relative to the current question and a higher goal. It is not a permanent rating of a memory fragment; it emerges from what that knowledge changes in our understanding and next justified action.

    02 / SYMBIOTIC SELF

    A person's intention, values and experience meet the agent's knowledge and ability to explore alternatives.     05 / TIME SERIES · MATHEMATICS · ML · RL

    Understand what is happening.
    Look ahead.

    Processes reveal themselves through changes over time. Pulse, breathing, current, temperature, price and the rate of completed work become observable parameters when we record what, when and how frequently we measure.

    A time series records how a process changes. We observe its recent history, identify states and relationships, then examine what might happen next: continued growth or decline, stabilization, or crossing a critical threshold. Comparing these scenarios against fresh observations allows us to estimate their likelihood and adjust the model. The practical result is the ability to notice an emerging change and prepare a decision before its consequences become obvious.

    FROM RELIABLE DATA TO TIMELY DECISIONS

    Understand the history to recognize changes in time.

    The reliability of a time series is ensured throughout its lifecycle: acquisition, storage, calculation, transmission and use. Systems monitor data integrity, detect deviations and automatically recover lost state from verified evidence. Regeneration and renewed checks return a consistent history to all dependent calculations. Antifragility is a key quality criterion: experience from failures initiates new checks and safeguards, reducing the chance that the same errors will recur.

    Explore data movement and recovery ↗
    Practical example · checking history coverage

    This foundation supports the study of process behavior:

    1. Reproducibility and controlled experimentsIdempotence guards against duplicated effects; deterministic calculations reproduce results with fixed data and conditions. Changing one parameter or method while keeping others comparable lets us examine its own contribution rather than the consequences of hidden technical changes.
    2. Behavior and stochastic variabilityA series reveals its final value and the path leading to it: states, sequences, behavioral patterns and transitions between modes. Classification and mathematical models help distinguish stable relationships from random fluctuations and understand the conditions where they apply.
    3. Predictivity and verifiable decisionsBased on observed relationships, we estimate possible future developments and the limits of a forecast. Subsequent observations test expectations; comparing correct decisions, mistakes and unknown outcomes refines both the model and the grounds for timely action.

    Reproducibility makes an experiment controllable; it does not remove randomness from the process being studied. The goal is to reduce uncertainty, recognize the grounds for effective action sooner and influence future outcomes.

    Example methodGPC · Gaussian processes ↗
    ONE EXAMPLE ANALYSIS METHOD
    GPC: from observed features
    to a probability of a state.

    Gaussian Process Classification helps distinguish predefined process states and estimate their probabilities based on observed features.

    Similarity and evidence. The model's kernel defines which states are similar. Labeled observations refine the distribution of a latent function used to derive class probabilities. The original measurements need not be normally distributed.

    Practical question. For example, is there a risk of failure in the next hour? Using previous incidents and features available at prediction time, the model estimates the probability of a specified event. Predicting the time to failure is a separate task; Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) deals with numerical estimates.

    Evaluating probabilities. Separate training and testing in time, avoid using future information as predictors, and compare the model with a simple baseline. Calibration tests whether predicted probabilities match the observed frequency of subsequent events. Decisions depend on both estimates and the consequences of error.
